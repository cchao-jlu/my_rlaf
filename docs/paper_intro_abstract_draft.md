## Abstract Draft

Learning-guided SAT solvers often face a practical tension. One-shot neural guidance is efficient because the neural model is queried only once before search, but it remains open-loop: it cannot react to what the CDCL solver observes during search. Fully dynamic neural branching can condition on solver state, but frequent neural calls inside the branching loop introduce substantial wall-clock overhead and can harm complete-solver reliability. This paper studies a conservative middle ground: low-frequency CDCL event traces are used to decide whether neural feedback should be activated at all.

We propose an Online-Consistent Selector for event-conditioned SAT guidance. The solver first obtains a one-shot neural guidance prior, runs short warmup probes to collect CDCL events, and then applies a conservative risk/recovery controller to choose between the one-shot path and an event-adapted guidance path. The selector is trained from same-evidence branch counterfactuals and is designed to recover hard or timeout instances while avoiding lost solutions and unnecessary slowdowns. As an ablation, we further study a guarded Local Boundary Correction that reopens a small set of over-conservative selector decisions under a candidate manifest.

On 200 held-out 3SAT-400 instances, the Online-Consistent Selector improves the one-shot neural guidance baseline from 50/200 to 56/200 solved instances and reduces mean wall-clock time from 47.752s to 46.222s. Local Boundary Correction preserves the 56/200 solved count but further reduces mean time to 45.498s, supporting its role as a boundary repair ablation rather than a separate primary method. These results suggest that sparse, event-conditioned, risk-controlled intervention is a practical way to use solver feedback without turning SAT solving into per-branching neural inference.

## Introduction Skeleton

### 1. Problem

Neural guidance has become a promising way to improve CDCL SAT solvers, but the integration point matters. A one-shot neural model can predict variable weights or polarities before search begins, keeping inference overhead low and preserving a simple solver workflow. This makes one-shot guidance attractive for wall-clock SAT solving. Its limitation is that the prediction is open-loop: once CDCL search starts, the neural policy does not observe conflicts, propagations, learnt clauses, activities, or other evidence generated by the solver.

At the other extreme, dynamic learned branching policies can condition on evolving solver state. This is more expressive, but it creates a practical cost and risk problem. Calling a neural model inside the branching loop can dominate runtime, and a learned heuristic can make a complete solver worse by losing instances that the baseline solves or by slowing easy instances enough to erase hard-instance gains. The paper starts from this tension:

> How can a SAT solver use real CDCL feedback without paying the cost and reliability risk of frequent neural branching?

### 2. Gap

Prior learning-guided SAT methods often fall into one of two regimes. Static methods use neural predictions before search and avoid repeated inference, but they do not use the observed search trajectory. Dynamic methods use solver state more directly, but their interaction with the solver can be expensive or harder to control under strict wall-clock evaluation.

The missing piece is not just a richer neural model. The missing piece is a conservative decision mechanism: after seeing a small amount of solver feedback, the system should decide whether feedback-conditioned neural guidance is actually worth using on this instance. This decision is asymmetric. A false positive can lose a solution or create a large slowdown; a false negative may miss a hard-instance speedup but keeps the safer one-shot path. The method therefore treats neural feedback as a risk-controlled intervention, not as a default replacement for the baseline solver path.

### 3. Method

The proposed workflow is low-frequency and evidence-driven:

1. Run the base GNN once to produce a one-shot neural guidance prior.
2. Run configured warmup probes under that prior and collect CDCL event traces.
3. Encode variable-level events and graph-level warmup statistics.
4. Use the Online-Consistent Selector to decide whether to activate event-conditioned guidance.
5. Optionally apply guarded Local Boundary Correction for a small set of over-conservative boundary cases.
6. Run the final CDCL solve with the selected guidance path.

The Online-Consistent Selector separates recovery evidence from risk evidence. It opens the adapter branch only when recovery potential is sufficiently high and slowdown or lost-solution risk is sufficiently low. This makes the main claim conservative: the goal is not to maximize adapter usage, but to recover hard instances while preserving the reliability of the one-shot path.

Local Boundary Correction is deliberately narrower. It is not a new global selector and not the main method. It only applies to a guarded candidate manifest and repairs a small number of `new_closed_old_on` boundary decisions where the conservative selector closes a useful adapter branch. The official full400 boundary correction uses the 750-feature guarded rule; no750 remains diagnostic only.

### 4. Result

The main full400 result supports the conservative-selector story:

| Method | Solved / 200 | Mean time |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| One-shot | 50 | 47.752s |
| Old Compact | 56 | 46.348s |
| Online-Consistent Selector | 56 | 46.222s |
| + Local Boundary Correction | 56 | 45.498s |

The Online-Consistent Selector recovers six additional instances over the one-shot neural guidance baseline and slightly improves mean time over the old compact reference. Local Boundary Correction does not increase solved count, but lowers mean time further by repairing a few conservative boundary decisions. This is why the paper should present Online-Consistent Selector as the main line and Local Boundary Correction as a boundary correction ablation.

### 5. Contributions

The contribution list should stay narrow:

1. We propose an event-conditioned online selector that uses real CDCL event traces to decide whether neural feedback should be activated.
2. We introduce conservative risk control for neural SAT guidance, separating recovery potential from slowdown and lost-solution risk.
3. We present guarded Local Boundary Correction, a constrained repair mechanism for over-conservative selector errors.

Do not list no750, polarity/SBE, compact stable, or pairwise veto as contributions. They belong in ablations or negative-results discussion.

## Opening Paragraph Draft

Modern CDCL SAT solvers are highly optimized, and even useful learned guidance can be harmful if it is inserted at the wrong point in the solving loop. One-shot neural guidance is attractive because it queries a GNN once before search and then lets the solver run normally, but this efficiency comes with an open-loop limitation: the guidance cannot react to conflicts, propagations, learnt clauses, or activity patterns observed during search. Dynamic neural branching can use such solver state, but frequent neural inference inside the branching loop creates wall-clock overhead and can introduce reliability risks for a complete solver. This paper asks whether solver feedback can be used more selectively: not to replace the branching heuristic at every decision, but to decide when neural feedback should be activated at all.
